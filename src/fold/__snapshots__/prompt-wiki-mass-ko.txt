  0: The simplest static black holes have mass but neither electric charge nor angular momentum.

[U1] paragraph
  0: While a black hole can theoretically have any positive mass, its charge and angular momentum are limited by its mass, with this limit being greater for more massive black holes.
  1: The net electric charge Q and the total angular momentum J satisfy the inequality {\frac {Q^{2}}{4\pi \epsilon _{0}}}+{\frac {c^{2}J^{2}}{GM^{2}}}\leq GM^{2} for a black hole of mass M, where \epsilon _{0} is the vacuum permittivity constant, c is the speed of light and G is the gravitational constant.
  2: Black holes with the maximum possible combination of charge and spin satisfying this inequality are called extremal black holes.
  3: Adding a low-mass object with a lot of charge or angular momentum to an extremal black hole would create a so-called naked singularity, a singularity outside of a black hole.
  4: Because these singularities make the universe inherently unpredictable, many physicists believe they could not exist.
  5: The weak cosmic censorship hypothesis, proposed by Penrose, rules out the formation of such singularities, when they are created through the gravitational collapse of realistic matter.
  6: This hypothesis remains an important area of study because has not yet been proven and it relates to many aspects of general relativity and quantum gravity.

[U2] paragraph
  0: The total mass of a nearby black hole can be estimated by analysing the motion of the stars or gas surrounding it.
  1: The mass of distant supermassive black holes can be inferred from Doppler broadening of spectral lines emitted by rapidly orbiting gas, a technique called reverberation mapping.
