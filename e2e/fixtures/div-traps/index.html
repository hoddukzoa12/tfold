The tide came in faster than the charts said it would, and by the time we reached the old pier the sand bar was already under a foot of water. The harbor master told us later that the spring tides this year run almost half an hour early because of the wind from the southwest.
우리는 해가 지기 전에 등대까지 걸어가기로 했지만, 바람이 점점 거세져서 결국 중간에 있는 작은 찻집에서 한 시간 넘게 기다려야 했다. 창밖으로 파도가 방파제를 넘는 모습을 보면서 다음에는 날씨를 먼저 확인하자고 약속했다.
We left the car at the top of the hill and walked down the old cart track, which the rain had turned into a shallow stream by the afternoon. Nobody minded, because the view over the bay was the clearest it had been all week.
The ferry was forty minutes late, so we sat on the sea wall and shared the last of the bread while the gulls circled. By the time it docked the sun had dropped behind the island and the water had gone from green to grey.
The harbor office marked a king tide on the fourteenth, the highest water of the whole year. Boat owners should check their lines and fenders the evening before.

The night ferry has a small cabin with eight bunks, and the crew hands out wool blankets once the boat leaves the breakwater. Tickets for the cabin sell out early, so book a bunk a week ahead in summer.

The night bus from the harbor stops at every village on the coast road, so a trip that takes forty minutes by car can take more than two hours after midnight
