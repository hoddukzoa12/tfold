What the tomatoes taught me this summer
By the gardener, on a rainy Sunday
I planted six tomato seedlings in late April, and by the end of July only four of them were still standing after the two summer storms that flattened the fence. The survivors were the ones I had tied to bamboo stakes early, before the stems grew heavy with fruit.
Watering turned out to matter more than feeding. On the weeks when I watered deeply twice instead of a little every evening, the leaves stayed green and the fruit split less often after rain.

A note for next year: the bed by the north wall gets two fewer hours of sun, so the cherry tomatoes should go there and the large ones should get the warm corner.
The notebook I kept by the back door, with dates, rainfall and the first ripe fruit of each plant,
turned out to be the most useful tool of the whole season, more than any gadget I bought.
Next spring I want to try two heirloom varieties from the seed swap at the library, and I will start them indoors three weeks earlier than this year.
