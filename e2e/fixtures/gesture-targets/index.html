The north pier

The north pier opened in the spring after two years of work on its pilings. Fishing boats moved over first, and the ferry followed them once the new ramp had passed its load tests. By the end of the summer the old pier stood empty, and the town began to argue about what to do with it.

Most of the argument was about money, as these arguments usually are. Tearing the old pier down would cost more than the town had set aside for it. Leaving it up would cost less this year and far more once the timbers began to rot, which the surveyors said would take about a decade.

What the survey found

The survey found that the southern third of the pier had kept its strength, while the northern end had lost a quarter of its load rating. That was enough to close the northern end to vehicles at once. It was not enough to settle the argument, because each side read the same figures as proof of what it had said all along. A year later the town voted to keep the southern third as a walkway and take down the rest. The work took one winter, and the timbers that could be saved went into the benches along the new walk. People who had argued for years now sit on those benches and argue about something else.
