The boatyard closes for two weeks in January while the slipway rails are replaced. Boats that need work before then should book a slot this week.

The ticket office moves to the old customs house for the winter while the pier building is repaired. The ferry times do not change.

The lighthouse museum reopens in March with a new room about the keepers and their families. Tickets are half price for children under twelve.

The sailing club lends life jackets to visitors who join a morning lesson on the bay. Bring a towel and a change of clothes.

The night ferry leaves from the east pier while the main pier is closed for repairs. Follow the lamps along the sea wall.

The spring regatta starts at noon on the first Sunday in May off the harbor mouth. Entries close a week before the race.

The harbor master wrote that the old fog horn on the breakwater will sound every thirty seconds whenever the fog comes in this winter. The horn is loud.

The council posted the full minutes of the winter meeting about the new harbor wall and the ferry pier on its board. Anyone can read them.

The fish market opens an hour later on Mondays so the boats can unload after the weekend. Ice is sold at the north door.

The tide table for March is pinned by the harbor office door. Spring tides fall on the fourth and the nineteenth.

The lifeboat crew trains in the bay on the first Wednesday of each month at dusk. Their flares are part of the drill.

The harbor choir sings on the quay on the last Sunday of August when the weather holds. Bring a chair and a warm coat.

The ice house on the old quay is open to visitors on Saturday mornings in the summer. Its walls are a yard thick.

The rowing club meets on the slipway at seven every Thursday evening from April to October. New rowers are welcome.
